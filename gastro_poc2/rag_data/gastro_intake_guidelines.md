# Gastroenterology Intake & Symptom-Questioning Guide

A reference for structured history-taking during pre-consultation intake. Use it
to decide which follow-up questions best narrow the differential. This is sample
seed content — replace with your clinic's own protocols.

## GI Alarm / Red-Flag Features (escalate, ask about explicitly)
- Unintentional weight loss
- Gastrointestinal bleeding: hematemesis (vomiting blood), melena (black tarry
  stools), or hematochezia (fresh blood per rectum)
- Iron-deficiency anemia / fatigue / pallor
- Progressive dysphagia (difficulty swallowing) or odynophagia (painful swallowing)
- Persistent vomiting
- Palpable abdominal mass or lymphadenopathy
- New-onset symptoms age > 50, or family history of GI cancer / IBD
- Nocturnal symptoms that wake the patient

## Abdominal Pain — questions to characterise (SOCRATES)
- Site: epigastric, right/left upper quadrant, peri-umbilical, lower quadrants, diffuse
- Onset: sudden vs gradual; what they were doing when it started
- Character: burning, cramping/colicky, dull/aching, sharp/stabbing
- Radiation: to back (pancreatic/biliary), to shoulder, around flank
- Associated: nausea, vomiting, fever, bloating, bowel changes
- Timing: constant vs intermittent; relation to meals (before/after), night
- Exacerbating/relieving: food, antacids, defecation, position, fatty meals
- Severity: 0–10 scale

## Dyspepsia / Reflux
- Heartburn: retrosternal burning, worse lying down or after fatty/large meals
- Regurgitation of acid or food
- Relation to meals; nocturnal symptoms; relief with antacids
- Differentiate functional dyspepsia (no alarm features) vs GERD vs PUD

## Bowel Habit Change
- Frequency change; consistency (Bristol Stool Scale 1–7)
- Diarrhea vs constipation vs alternating
- Mucus or blood in stool; urgency; tenesmus; incomplete evacuation
- Nocturnal diarrhea (suggests organic disease)
- Relation to specific foods (e.g., lactose, gluten)

## Nausea & Vomiting
- Timing (morning, post-prandial), content (bilious, blood, undigested food)
- Frequency and volume; relation to pain; presence of fever
- Associated weight loss or dehydration signs

## Liver / Biliary
- Jaundice (yellow eyes/skin), pale stools, dark urine, pruritus (itching)
- Right upper quadrant pain, especially after fatty meals (biliary colic)

## Relevant Habits to Probe
- Alcohol intake (units/week) — liver, pancreatitis, gastritis
- Smoking — PUD, malignancy risk
- NSAID / steroid / anticoagulant use — bleeding, ulcers
- Diet, recent travel, antibiotic use (infectious causes)

## Common Differentials Mapped to Patterns (for question targeting, not diagnosis)
- Epigastric burning, meal-related, no alarm features → functional dyspepsia / GERD / PUD
- RUQ pain after fatty meals → biliary colic / gallstones
- Epigastric pain radiating to back, heavy alcohol → pancreatitis
- Altered bowel habit + blood + weight loss, older patient → colorectal pathology (urgent)
- Chronic diarrhea + weight loss + young patient → IBD / malabsorption / coeliac
